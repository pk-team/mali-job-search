Explanation practice

Answer the question, then reveal the understanding.

A good answer is not a memorized paragraph. It has layers, so Mali can stop after a clear sentence or continue into trade-offs, a concrete example, and an honest boundary.

The three-depth answer

15s

Direct meaning

One or two sentences that answer exactly what was asked in ordinary language.

60s

Purpose and trade-off

Why teams use it, where it sits, and one cost or alternative.

deep

Evidence and boundary

A real example, what Mali did personally, what she did not own, what went wrong, and what she learned.

Example: What is gRPC?

15 seconds

gRPC is a way for applications or services to call each other using a strongly defined contract, often with Protocol Buffers. In an app, I would usually generate a client and hide it behind a service interface.

One minute

It solves a similar communication problem to a REST API, but emphasizes generated, strongly typed contracts and supports efficient binary messages and streaming. That can be useful between backend services. The trade-off is that it is less immediately inspectable in a browser and adds contract/tooling decisions. Most application code should depend on a capability such as IOrdersGateway, not on gRPC everywhere.

Honest learning boundary

I have not operated gRPC in production. I would demonstrate it in a small .NET project by implementing the same gateway with gRPC and REST, then show that the UI and business rule do not change.

Project-story questions

  1. What problem was the user trying to solve?
  2. What exactly did you own?
  3. What information or guidance did you have?
  4. What design or implementation decision did you make, and why?
  5. What was difficult or failed?
  6. How did you know the result worked?
  7. What did you not own?
  8. What would you improve now?

Questions that reveal mentorship

  • How does a junior developer learn the wider system rather than only receive tickets?
  • Who reviews frontend code and design decisions, and what does that review look like?
  • Can you describe the first complete piece of work a new junior might own?
  • How are mistakes surfaced and discussed?
  • How often do developers demo work or explain technical decisions?
  • What would you expect someone in this role to understand after three months?
