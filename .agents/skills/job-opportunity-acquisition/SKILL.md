# Job opportunity acquisition

Build a small, deliberately varied sample of real opportunities. The purpose is to learn what the market is asking for and discover useful routes—not to maximize listing count or pretend that a keyword match predicts a good job.

Read `evidence/mali-baseline.html`, [references/sampling-and-triage.md](references/sampling-and-triage.md), and the search lanes in `../bangkok-opportunity-search/references/search-lanes.md`.

## Acquisition principles

- Search for work patterns and user problems as well as titles and technologies. Include frontend, UI engineering, product engineering, implementation, junior full-stack, trainee, apprenticeship, and credible adjacent routes when supported by the request.
- Sample across different access routes, company shapes, seniority signals, and technology clusters. Do not let one job board, title, or framework define the market.
- Treat a mismatch as data. A posting can be poor for immediate application and excellent for learning, language practice, company research, or discovering people and communities.
- Do not silently discard a posting because it mentions a degree, years of experience, unfamiliar buzzwords, a recruiter, or an imperfect title. Record the signal and place the opportunity in an appropriate lane.
- Exclude from active pursuit only for a stated reason such as an expired/unverifiable source, a confirmed legal or location barrier, a clear scam/safety concern, or work that conflicts with the user's stated boundaries. Preserve a minimal archive record when the listing still teaches something.
- Never infer culture, professionalism, ownership, accessibility, or HR behavior from wording or company identity alone.
- Do not contact, register, apply, upload personal details, or start a paid trial without explicit authorization.

## Workflow

1. Define a sampling question, such as “What Bangkok roles could value frontend evidence?” or “How are .NET teams describing junior-adjacent work?”
2. Acquire a batch of 5–8 specific, current postings across at least three useful variations. A smaller batch is acceptable when sources are sparse.
3. Preserve for each item: URL, access date, title, stated company, location/work arrangement, source type, posting freshness, and a concise summary.
4. Run a shallow first pass: likely user/problem, recurring terms, strongest gate signal, evidence adjacency, learning yield, human-access clue, and major unknown.
5. Assign an exploration lane: `pursue`, `network-first`, `learning-rich`, `watch`, or `archive`. This is routing, not a verdict on Mali or the employer.
6. Choose a diverse subset for full dossiers. Use `job-posting-analysis` for each selected posting; do not duplicate its company research or detailed scoring here.
7. Compare the batch: repeated concepts, outlier concepts, access patterns, recurring gates, and which one or two learning projects would transfer across several jobs.
8. Update `jobs/index.html` with a dated acquisition batch and links to completed dossiers. Mark time-sensitive results for refresh.

## Output quality

Explain why each item was retained and what it can teach even when application fit is weak. Make uncertainty visible. Prefer a small batch with useful variation and durable source capture over a long ranked list.
