# Inference discipline

## Confidence labels

- Verified: directly supported by source, behavior, contract, or a primary stakeholder statement.
- Reported: supplied by a person but not yet corroborated by an artifact or independent source.
- Strong inference: several distinctive facts converge and alternatives are weak.
- Likely: consistent with evidence and normal practice, but credible alternatives remain.
- Speculative: useful hypothesis with little discriminating evidence.
- Unknown: no responsible conclusion yet.

## Client-goal ladder

Do not stop at “build a screen.” Test hypotheses at three levels:

1. User outcome: what becomes easier, safer, faster, or possible for the person using it?
2. Operational outcome: what work, delay, error, exception, or coordination cost changes?
3. Business outcome: what revenue, retention, trust, compliance, risk, or strategic capability changes?

For each goal, ask what observable measure would move if the hypothesis were true.

## Challenge every model

- What else could produce the same code shape?
- Which important actor is absent from the interface?
- Where does money, authority, or irreversible state change?
- Who handles failure after the visible UI says success?
- What might the client have optimized that harms another actor?
- What evidence would disprove the preferred story?

Prefer useful uncertainty over a vivid but unsupported narrative.
