Understand · experiment · explain · connect · choose

Increase the number of good futures available.

Every project, job advert, conversation, system reconstruction, and carefully bounded experiment can create new options. The point is not to chase everything. It is to understand more deeply, produce credible evidence, and make better choices—including the choice to walk away.

The working thesis: knowledge creates options when it changes what Mali can notice, build, explain, ask, or choose. She should not compete by pretending to have conventional credentials; she should make judgement, curiosity, independent ability, and growth unusually easy to see.

The system

01

See the whole system

Infer the users, incentives, flows, failures, constraints, and client goals that sit behind the visible code.

 02

Acquire and decode opportunities

Sample broadly, route rather than silently reject, and ask what the advert, company, recruiter, interviewer, and actual work each reveal—and conceal.

 03

Deflate the buzzwords

Explain each term as an ordinary engineering choice: problem, trade-off, example, and boundary.

 04

Create evidence

Build one visible slice that clarifies an idea, a user need, or a useful comparison.

 05

Run bounded experiments

Try new agents, frameworks, and workflows with a question, safety boundary, uninstall path, and learning record.

 06

Practise perspective

Explain clearly and ask questions suited to HR, a technical lead, a manager, a founder, or a future colleague.

 07

Open multiple paths

Compare jobs, internships, apprenticeships, communities, international work, contracts, and collaborations.

 08

Protect credibility

Record what is proven, reported, inferred, or unknown. Never let ambition turn into an inflated claim.

What success looks like

The near-term goal is not “submit the most applications.” It is to increase the number and quality of credible next moves available to Mali while improving her ability to judge them.

Learning

She can explain a previously intimidating term and show it in a small working system.

Evidence

Each portfolio claim points to code, a design artifact, a commit, a screenshot, or a candid project note.

Access

More conversations occur with engineers, founders, designers, and community organizers—not only anonymous portals.

Agency

She chooses opportunities based on learning, support, and real work—not the prestige of a buzzword list.

Judgement

She can recognize a dead end, a good stretch, a missing mentor, a hidden business goal, and a question worth asking.
