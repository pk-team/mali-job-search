Systems thinking · initial reconstruction 24 August 2026

Code is evidence of a larger human system.

Mali was given interface work without much business or architectural context. We can recover part of that missing mentorship by asking what users were trying to accomplish, what the client was buying, where the application sat, what could fail, and how success might have been measured.

Inference discipline: these are models, not recovered truth. Each claim is labelled as verified, reported, inferred, or unknown. Understanding the wider system does not imply Mali designed or owned it.

The reconstruction lens

Purpose

What change in the real world justified paying for this software?

People

Who uses, buys, operates, supports, approves, or is affected by it?

Flow

What triggers the work, what state changes, and what must happen next?

Risk

What failure costs money, time, trust, safety, or regulatory exposure?

Measure

What observable result would convince the client the application helped?

Boundary

What does this code own, and what remains upstream, downstream, or invisible?

System hypothesis 1: airline gift vouchers

Verified in source The application has a multi-stage shopping flow, voucher products, purchaser and recipient details, payment handling, airline voucher generation, gift-certificate email delivery, and administrative reporting. Voucher rules include value, currency, expiry, permitted markets/flights, fare classes, date restrictions, transferability, taxes, fees, and single-use behavior.

Likely system

StageLikely responsibilityConfidence
MerchandisingDefine voucher products, denominations, eligibility, and localized content.Verified shape
PurchaseBuyer chooses value/quantity and supplies recipient identity, email, and message.Verified
PaymentExternal payment result is validated and the order is marked paid.Verified
IssuancePaid order items become vouchers in the airline reservation/payment system.Verified
DeliveryRecipient receives an email containing value, expiry, and redemption information.Verified
Redemption and accountingVoucher is later applied to eligible travel; liability and unused value must be tracked somewhere.Strong inference

What the client probably wanted

  • Strong inference Sell future travel as a gift product without staff manually issuing and emailing certificates.
  • Likely Receive cash before travel while creating a future booking and possibly a new customer.
  • Likely Let purchaser and traveller be different people, including a personal message and localized communication.
  • Likely Preserve airline control over routes, dates, fare classes, taxes, transferability, and expiry.
  • Likely Give finance/support staff reports linking orders, payments, recipients, and issued vouchers.

Valuable unknowns

  • Was the primary goal incremental revenue, gifting/brand reach, customer acquisition, or reducing manual work?
  • Who owned fraud, refunds, failed issuance after successful payment, duplicate callbacks, and voucher liability?
  • Were recipient delivery rate, conversion to booking, time-to-redeem, breakage, support contacts, or purchase completion measured?
  • Which screens and design decisions did the airline specifically request, and what user research existed?

Interview-safe explanation

Short version

“I worked on the customer-facing HTML, CSS, components, and design of an airline gift-voucher flow. Looking at the wider system, the interface sat between a purchaser, payment processing, voucher issuance in the airline system, recipient email, and later redemption. I did not own those backend integrations, but understanding them helps me explain why payment and delivery states mattered in the UI.”

System hypothesis 2: aircraft-change reseating

Verified in source ReseatingUI is an operator console over a backend queue. After an aircraft or cabin-configuration change, another system proposes passenger seat changes. Staff can search, inspect, process immediately, deactivate, or reschedule entries individually or in bulk. The UI does not contain the seat-mapping algorithm and does not itself apply changes to the passenger-service system.

Likely system

StageLikely responsibilityConfidence
Aircraft assignmentOperations changes the equipment/configuration for a scheduled flight.Verified shape
DetectionA backend notices the swap and creates an equipment-change record.Strong inference
MappingRules propose a destination seat for every affected passenger.Verified shape
Exception controlStaff review urgency and exceptions, then process, defer, or cancel queued changes.Verified
ExecutionA worker changes seats in the passenger-service system and records outcomes.Strong inference
CommunicationPassengers are notified by an unseen downstream channel.Likely

What the client probably wanted

  • Strong inference Reduce the time and manual labour required after an aircraft swap affects many seat assignments.
  • Strong inference Preserve passenger expectations as far as possible while handling cases automation cannot safely resolve.
  • Likely Prevent rushed, inconsistent, or unauthorised mass changes through permissions, scheduling, reporting, and explicit operator intervention.
  • Likely Give operations staff a compact view of urgent work and bulk controls under time pressure.

Valuable unknowns

  • Who are the actual operators: airline operations, airport staff, reservations, or a vendor support team?
  • What is the service-level target between aircraft change, new seats, and passenger notification?
  • Which outcomes matter most: preserved seat attributes, completion rate, fewer support calls, speed, or reduced denied boarding/conflict?
  • How are families, accessibility needs, paid seats, infants, status passengers, and failed assignments prioritized?
  • Why can the visible UI not override an individual destination seat even though the API exposes that capability?

Interview-safe explanation

Short version

“I worked on the interface and reusable components for a reseating operations tool. The larger problem was not choosing seats in the UI; it was supervising an automated queue after an aircraft change invalidated existing seat assignments. My work needed to make large result sets, selection, urgency, loading, and bulk actions understandable. I did not build the mapping algorithm or airline backend.”
