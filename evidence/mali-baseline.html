Safe claims now

  • Junior frontend developer with practical Blazor/Razor component, HTML, CSS, responsive-interface, and API-integration experience.
  • Has worked independently across substantial interface changes and reusable components.
  • Has design sensitivity supported by implementation evidence; Figma and meeting participation should be described as reported until artifacts are collected.
  • Has adjacent full-stack learning evidence in public SvelteKit, TypeScript, GraphQL, ASP.NET Core, and PostgreSQL projects, subject to per-project verification when used.

Claims to avoid

  • Do not claim a degree, conventional school credential, seniority, or a formal role title that cannot be evidenced.
  • Do not claim ownership of Arajet's backend, database, service architecture, or production operation.
  • Do not turn commit count into quality, business impact, or sole authorship.
  • Do not call independent learning “production experience” unless the specific project actually served users in production.

The development gap

The strongest gap is not raw ability; it is structured understanding and articulation. The programme should repeatedly ask: Who was the user? What were they trying to accomplish? What part did Mali own? Why did she choose this design? What failed? What trade-off did she make? What would she change now?

Mentorship requirements for a first role

System context

Someone explains the product, users, architecture, and why work matters.

Review

Regular design and code review with reasons, not silent acceptance.

Increasing scope

Small complete ownership first, followed by deliberate expansion.

Explanation

Opportunities to demo, write notes, ask questions, and describe decisions.
