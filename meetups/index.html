Community route

Do not “network.” Become a familiar, useful participant.

The goal is not to ask strangers for jobs. It is to learn in public, meet people repeatedly, contribute at a manageable scale, and create enough context that opportunities and introductions become natural.

Current event list intentionally pending. Bangkok meetup schedules change quickly. A live, dated research pass should be done before recommending specific events; inactive groups and old calendars must not be presented as current.

What to look for

Repeat cadence

A real recurring community is more valuable than a large one-off conference.

Beginner permeability

Look for project nights, lightning talks, study groups, open source, design/frontend sessions, or explicit newcomer support.

Relevant people

Engineers, designers, founders, maintainers, and technical educators—not only recruitment booths.

Safe participation

Public venue, clear organizer identity and code of conduct, practical travel, and no pressure to share personal information.

A six-visit progression

  1. Observe. Learn the format; have two genuine conversations.
  2. Return. Recognize people, ask one informed question, thank the organizer.
  3. Help. Offer a small task: notes, setup, design feedback, or welcoming another newcomer.
  4. Share. Show a two-minute project slice or ask for focused feedback.
  5. Contribute. Give a five-minute explanation such as “REST, GraphQL, and gRPC without the drama.”
  6. Connect. Follow up with specific thanks, an artifact, or an invitation to continue a technical discussion.
