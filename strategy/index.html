Operating philosophy

Several paths, one accumulating body of evidence.

Job boards, direct outreach, portfolio projects, meetups, and interview practice are not competing plans. They are connected experiments. Every path should improve Mali's understanding and leave something useful behind.

The seven lanes

1

Evidence reconstruction

Turn Arajet, ReseatingUI, Figma work, meetings, and independent projects into truthful stories: user problem, responsibility, decision, result, limitation, and lesson.

2

Opportunity intelligence

Use listings as market signals. Track repeated technologies, product domains, seniority language, degree filters, and whether the posting appears close to the engineering team.

3

Learning projects

Build the smallest complete workflow that makes the job's central concepts visible. Prefer one finished vertical slice over a broad imitation of the employer's product.

4

Explanation practice

Learn to answer at three depths. Start with the plain meaning, explain the trade-off, then connect to a genuine example or state that it is a learning example.

5

Direct access

Find founders, engineering leaders, designers, open-source maintainers, and small product teams where demonstrated work can be evaluated before a credential checklist.

6

Community

Attend repeatedly, contribute something small, ask good questions, follow up, and eventually show work. Familiarity and usefulness create referrals more reliably than one-off networking.

7

Mentorship test

Evaluate whether a role offers review, feedback, system context, and increasing responsibility. A first job without guidance can repeat the exact weakness this programme is trying to repair.

Credential-gate heuristic

This is a probability estimate, never a verdict. A bachelor's requirement alone does not prove rejection, and a friendly startup tone does not prove direct access.

Signals of a stronger gate

  • Degree is repeated or described as mandatory.
  • Long, generic years-and-keywords checklist.
  • Large corporate portal or unnamed recruiter.
  • No portfolio, equivalent experience, or work-sample route.
  • Wording is generic across unrelated technical roles.

Signals of a more permeable route

  • Named product, team, engineering problem, or hiring manager.
  • Portfolio, demonstrable skill, or equivalent experience welcomed.
  • Small technical team and direct contact path.
  • Specific craft expectations and realistic scope.
  • Community presence, open source, talks, or visible technical leadership.
