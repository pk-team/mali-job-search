A remote job, an internship, and an apprenticeship are different doors.

Each route has its own legal, educational, evidence, experience, and relationship gates. Optionality improves when we identify the exact door rather than repeatedly pushing on one labelled “job.”

Bangkok internships: viable, but often structurally gated

Current programmes show that substantial engineering internships exist in Bangkok. LINE advertises a ten-week software engineering internship with product features, tests, mentorship, Agile/Scrum, frontend/backend knowledge, Git, teamwork, and learning ability. Its visible listing does not state a degree requirement. LINE Software Engineering Intern

Agoda runs paid Bangkok internships, but its student page explicitly welcomes people currently enrolled in a college, university, or graduate programme. That makes many of its internships unavailable to Mali because of programme design, not because her work is irrelevant. Agoda student programmes

Conclusion: search internships, but inspect eligibility first. Also search for trainee, junior, apprenticeship, fellowship, project assistant, paid trial, fixed-term project, open-source mentorship, and direct small-team arrangements that do not require university enrollment.

United States: no degree is possible, but geography is a separate gate

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics says software-developer roles typically require a bachelor's degree. For web developers and digital designers, however, educational requirements vary from a high-school diploma to a bachelor's degree. That makes Mali's interface/design evidence more permeable than a generic “software engineer” funnel, though individual employers still set their own rules. What globally distributed companies visibly value

Current examples prove that Thailand is not automatically excluded. Metabase advertises a global-remote frontend role and describes a team spanning “Thailand to California.” It explicitly says it is not picky about a specific degree, but asks for unusually strong React/JavaScript, product sensibility, tests, clear proposals, CSS/design systems, and experience in a large codebase. Automattic hires worldwide and uses detailed written applications plus a paid trial; its current engineering role asks for production experience, quality, debugging, systems layers, abstraction trade-offs, collaboration, and independent remote work. These are evidence of a route, not evidence that either current role is junior-suitable. Metabase frontend role · Automattic engineering role · Automattic hiring process

Visible product judgement

Interfaces that solve a real user's problem, including failure and edge states.

Written communication

Clear asynchronous explanations, decisions, questions, and progress updates.

Systems thinking

Understanding layers above and below the immediate code and the cost of abstractions.

Independent execution

Ability to investigate and finish without disappearing or pretending not to need feedback.

Collaboration evidence

Reviews, issues, pull requests, design discussions, open source, and responsiveness.

Proof over claims

A small number of deployed, documented, tested projects that can survive close questions.

Practical route order

  1. Build systems explanations around the two real client projects.
  2. Curate two or three public projects for finish, documentation, tests, and demonstration.
  3. Contribute to a relevant open-source or community project where collaboration is visible.
  4. Search Bangkok junior/trainee/direct-team routes and non-student-gated programmes.
  5. Track globally distributed companies and study what their application processes reward.
  6. Apply internationally only when the role explicitly supports Thailand or worldwide hiring and the evidence threshold is credible.

Community is a separate path with its own method. See Meetups: become a familiar, useful participant.

This is route research, not legal, immigration, tax, or employment advice. Verify the arrangement for each actual offer.
